THE DESIGN IN ONE PARAGRAPH

A long, narrow bar laid along the contour rather than across it — the move that minimises cut and fill on steep ground. Glass and terraces face downhill to the south-southeast, where the view and the winter sun arrive from the same direction; the uphill wall is nearly solid and holds an 11′-0″ service spine carrying every wet room, every chase and all mechanical distribution. Three levels step with the hill: a walkout lower level the slope gives you almost for free, a main level with everything essential on one floor, and a sleeping level under a 3:12 shed. A reinforced concrete spine wall retains the cut, carries the structure, resists lateral load and stores heat — one element doing four jobs, where the conventional alternative builds a retaining wall and a frame in front of it and pays twice. The roofs fall downhill, so no roof water is ever delivered to the uphill side where the cut and the groundwater problem already are. A 4′-0″ drained drain gap runs the length of the uphill wall so the house never touches the cut face. The garage is detached, joined by a conditioned mudroom link and a 9′-0″ covered breezeway, which removes garage-to-house fire separation and CO migration at the source.

SYSTEMS — 232 ROUTED RUNS

Generated from the fixture schedule and routed orthogonally through the service spine and two vertical chases. Every run answers: where does it come from, where does it go, what happens when it fails.

SystemBodyThe move that mattersOn failure
WATERArterial One pump, home-run branches from a single manifold in The Heart. A gravity branch bypasses the pump entirely — ~9.5 psi static from the cistern, so power out still gives you a tap and a WC.
WASTEVenous Gravity, converging, never crossing the supply side. Water in at the top, waste out at the bottom. The pressure main to the field often runs uphill on steep ground, so a pump failure backs sewage up — the high-water float is hardwired and audible, not an app notification.
AIRAirway Ventilation ducted independently of heating — supply to bedrooms, exhaust from wet rooms, continuous and low-volume. You never re-breathe through the furnace, and ventilation does not stop when the heat is satisfied.
POWERNerves Home runs to one brain in The Heart; battery then generator behind a critical-loads panel. Reflex arcs (shown red) run without the brain: leak sensor → motorised shutoff, freeze stat, septic float. Any safety function that needs the internet is not a safety function.

IT IS TESTED

node tools/check/clash.mjs     →  14 passed, 1 warning, 0 failed

The checker interrogates the model rather than the drawing. On its first run it found 16 failures, several structural: bedroom 3 was reachable only through bath 2; the mechanical room's only door opened into bath 3; bedroom 2's door sat inside the room rather than on a wall; the linen closet had no door; the electrical panel had no NEC working space; five door swings landed on fixtures; bath 2 needed 82″ of depth in a 76″ room. A later run caught the terrace stair standing in front of the guest bedroom's escape window, which is a life-safety fault rather than a clash and now has its own check.
