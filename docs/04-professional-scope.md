# 04 — WHAT REQUIRES A LICENSED PROFESSIONAL

Nothing in this package is engineered. Every member size, pipe diameter, duct size and equipment capacity is a **coordination placeholder** — geometry chosen to reserve realistic space and to prove the systems can physically coexist. None of it is a design value.

This document says who must produce the real values, and what would be negligent to build without them.

---

## A. Must be sealed before construction

| Discipline | Professional (NC licensed) | Scope | Why it cannot be skipped here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Geotechnical** | Geotechnical Engineer, PE | Borings/test pits, bearing capacity, **global slope stability**, groundwater, rock depth, retaining wall design parameters, landslide/debris-flow hazard | Colluvial soils are common on Blue Ridge slopes and are frequently unsuitable. A slope-stability failure is a life-safety event, not a cost event. **This governs whether the site is buildable at all.** |
| **Structural** | Structural Engineer, PE | The concrete spine/retaining wall, all ribs, the downhill column line, foundations, the elevated deck and entry bridge, and the **complete lateral system** | Steep sites routinely exceed prescriptive wall bracing — tall cripple walls, stepped foundations, an almost fully glazed downhill wall. This design assumes engineered lateral design, not prescriptive bracing. Design loads (snow, wind exposure/Kzt, seismic) are **not established** — see `02-code-basis.md` §D. |
| **On-site wastewater** | Licensed Soil Scientist + NC-licensed On-Site Wastewater System Designer | Soil evaluation, system type, sizing, dispersal field siting, repair area, pump and dosing design | Steep slopes usually exclude conventional trenches. **Commission this first** — it can veto the site. |
| **Surveying** | NC Professional Land Surveyor | Boundary, 2 ft topography, easements, location of the existing water source, road frontage, floodplain | Every geometric assumption in the model depends on it. |
| **Civil / stormwater / erosion** | Civil Engineer, PE | Driveway geometry and drainage, culverts, level spreaders, erosion & sedimentation control plan, post-construction stormwater | Rainfall intensity (NOAA Atlas 14) is **not established**, so no conveyance in this package is sized. |
| **Architecture** | Architect, NC licensed | Code compliance, life safety, egress, assemblies, construction documents, contract administration | See §C on when NC law requires this. |

| Discipline | Professional | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| **MEP** | Mechanical/Electrical/Plumbing Engineer or qualified designer | Manual J/S/D load calculations and equipment selection, duct design, ERV sizing and balancing, electrical load calculation and service sizing, water treatment train, pump/pressure design |

## C. On the requirement for a licensed architect in North Carolina

⚫ **NOT ESTABLISHED.** NC statute generally exempts certain single-family residences from mandatory architectural sealing, but the exemption's scope and thresholds were **not verified** — this environment had no access to `ncleg.gov` or the NC Board of Architecture.

Two things are true regardless of the exemption:

1. An exemption from *sealing* is not an exemption from *the code*. The house must still comply.
2. This particular house — a three-level stepped structure on a steep slope with a structural retaining spine, an engineered lateral system, and an elevated bridge and deck — is exactly the case where the exemption is least appropriate even where it legally applies.

**Verify with Watauga County Planning & Inspections and the NC Board of Architecture.**

## D. Sequence — and why order matters

The first three can invalidate the design. Do not pay for documentation before them.

1. **Wastewater soil evaluation** → can veto the site or relocate the house
2. **Boundary + topographic survey** → replaces every geometric assumption
3. **Geotechnical investigation** → can change the entire foundation strategy
4. Water source evaluation → determines whether the gravity-backup strategy survives
5. Jurisdiction + zoning confirmation by parcel ID
6. Design loads (ASCE 7 Hazard Tool at the surveyed coordinates) + AHJ snow load confirmation
7. Structural, civil and MEP design
8. Construction documents

Running 7 before 1–3 is the most common and most expensive mistake made on steep mountain sites.
