# 07 — SEVEN SCHEMES

Driven by the two reference packs. The instruction at the foot of the blueprint wall is the whole method:

> **Do not ask whether HENRY resembles these houses.** Put HENRY's plan and section beside them at the same scale. Compare conditioned area, sheltered area, perimeter, wet-wall length, foundations, roof intersections, ground contacts, rooms served, future capacity, and cost.

So `model/schemes.mjs` builds seven alternatives in the same site frame, over the same hill, and computes exactly that list. **X-101** is the sheet. `out/schemes/` holds a render of each from one fixed camera.

---

## A. THE TWO BARS, KEPT SEPARATE

**Rural Studio / Front Porch is the BUILD-INTELLIGENCE bar** — phasing, kit-of-parts, minimum viable dwelling, structure as architecture, utility concentration, roof and porch and water as one system. What it contributes is measurable discipline. The Front Porch product line publishes its numbers, and they are brutal:

|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Dave's House | 504 sf | 14 × 44 | 128 sf | 100 lf | 0.198 |
| MacArthur's House | 536 sf | 18 × 38 | 100 sf | 120 lf | 0.224 |
| Joanne's House | 529 sf | 26 × 29 | 204 sf | 104 lf | 0.197 |
| Sylvia 2/1 | 856 sf | 22 × 48 | 206 sf | 140 lf | 0.164 |
| Sylvia 2/2 | 1,024 sf | 24 × 52 | 224 sf | 152 lf | 0.148 |
| Sylvia 3/2 | 1,364 sf | 28 × 58 | 256 sf | 172 lf | 0.126 |

**Sylvia 2/1 matters most.** It is a product-line house actually adapted to a narrow, steeply sloped site in **Madison County, North Carolina** — two counties from Watauga, the same mountains, the same problem. 856 sf.

**Olson Kundig's mountain and forest work is the VISUAL AND SECTIONAL bar** — tiny footprint with monumental presence, minimal ground contact, one great roof or frame or opening, raw material, and resilience expressed as an architectural **state** rather than as equipment hidden in a closet. Sol Duc is 350 sf. Vermont Cabin is 750 sf. Knob Hill is the sectional lesson: concrete rooted into the slope, light volume above.

The current design is **3,747 sf.**

That is the number the reference pack is really pointing at, and no amount of coordination quality answers it.

## B. THE SEVEN

Each names one **transferable operation** and one thing it **must not copy**. These are other people's built work: the operation travels, the form does not.

### S0 — THE SPINE · *the opponent*
The current design. A concrete spine retains the cut, carries every rib, resists lateral load and stores heat — one element doing four jobs. It keeps the systems intelligence of the whole package. It must be beaten on mass, phasing, cost, ground relationship and clarity, or kept for stated reasons.

### S1 — THE ARMATURE · *from Rev. Walker's Home*
**Operation:** build the durable thing first — a five-bay frame and one roof standing over the slope. Enclose two bays now. The rest is covered territory that becomes rooms later *without cutting into the original roof*.
**Do not copy:** the Alabama vernacular, the gable, the porch detailing. The transferable thing is the sequence.
**Result:** Phase 1 is 616 sf and moves **8 CY of earth**. Mature is 1,540 sf — **+150% without demolition**, the best growth capacity of the seven. It also has the worst envelope efficiency (0.299 lf/sf), which is the honest price of standing a big roof over a small house on day one.

### S2 — THE BRIDGE · *from 20K Truss Home and 20K Bridge Home*
**Operation:** let the structure span, so the building touches the ground in six places instead of along a continuous wall.
**Do not copy:** the 20K cost model. This is a mountain house with mountain loads.
**Result:** 1,200 sf on **six piers, 9 CY**. A dry undercroft under the span is a later level for free.

### S3 — THE NARROW · *from Geraldine's Home and Sylvia 2/1*
**Operation:** a body narrow enough to daylight and cross-ventilate from both sides, a service bar down the uphill edge, and a dogtrot cut through the middle that costs nothing and does everything.
**Do not copy:** the product-line plan itself — this site has 30% cross-slope and snow.
**Result:** the most conventional house of the alternatives at 1,716 sf mature, and the most expensive of them in earth at 253 CY. It answers the question the wall asks — *how narrow before it stops being generous* — at 22 ft, which is what Sylvia 2/1 used on a steep NC site.

### S4 — THE CORE · *from Myers' Home*
**Operation:** concentrate every expensive and fixed thing — stair, plumbing, flue, mechanical, structure — into one small permanent core. Everything around it is light, cheap and re-plannable without touching it.
**Do not copy:** the attic truss and the shell proportions. The transferable thing is the **division**: permanent versus transient.
**Result:** 36 lf of wet wall against the Spine's 87.

### S5 — THE PERCH · *from Sol Duc Cabin and Vermont Cabin*
**Operation:** refuse the horizontal. One small footprint, lifted clear of the ground, stacked vertically, one enormous opening on the downhill face, and a shutter that closes the whole thing when nobody is there.
**Do not copy:** the steel-plate aesthetic and the hand-crank theatre. Copy the tiny ground contact, the vertical stack, and resilience as a **state**.
**Result:** the striking one. **1,872 sf behind 100 lf of perimeter — 0.053 lf per sf, the best of the seven — and 17 CY of earth.** The Spine needs 226 lf and 440 CY.

### S6 — THE TOWER · *from the 18 × 18 House*
**Operation:** if the buildable shelf is small, vertical growth is cheaper than long foundations. One 18 ft square, four levels, one stair, one stack.
**Do not copy:** nothing formal. This scheme exists to falsify the long bar, and earns its place only if the numbers say so.
**Result:** **72 lf of perimeter and 18 lf of wet wall** — the leanest of all — for 1,296 sf, doubling from a 648 sf Phase 1.

## C. WHAT THE NUMBERS SAY SO FAR

Read the sheet, not this paragraph. But three things are already clear:

**1. The earthwork gap is not marginal, it is categorical.** The Spine moves **440 CY** for its building bench alone — before the motor court and the 655 ft driveway, which every scheme still needs. The Armature moves **8**. The Perch moves **17**. On a site where docs/06 already reports 5,845 CY of net spoil and no designed place to put it, that is the most consequential column in the table.

**2. Perimeter is where Rural Studio's discipline bites.** The Spine spends **226 linear feet of exterior wall** on 3,747 sf. The Perch spends 100 on 1,872 — the same ratio, half the house. The Tower spends 72. Exterior wall is envelope, insulation, cladding, flashing, air-sealing and heat loss, in that order, forever.

**3. Nothing here has been costed, and that changes the ranking.** The Bridge's trusses are not free; the piers it saves are cheap holes but the span above them is not. The Armature's roof is built at full size on day one and paid for by a 616 sf house. The Tower needs a stair through four floors and a crane. **The next move is to price all seven off the same unit rates in `model/cost.mjs`, because until that runs, "least earth" is not the same as "least money."**

---

## D. WHAT THIS IS NOT

- **Not a recommendation.** Seven propositions, one opponent, one computed table.
- **Not detailed.** The 3D is massing only, unfurnished and unglazed by intent — at this stage detail would flatter whichever scheme got detailed first.
- **Not programme-matched.** The Spine holds four bedrooms and an office. Most alternatives hold two or three. **A fair comparison has to answer whether Henry needs 3,747 sf at all** — and that is a question for Henry, not for the model.
- **Not verified.** Same standing caveats as the rest of the package: no survey, no soils, no code text, nothing engineered.
